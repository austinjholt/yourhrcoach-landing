How to Get HR Help When You Don't Have an HR Department

An honest comparison of the five ways small businesses get HR support — what each costs, what each is good at, and how to choose.

By Dr. Steve Cohen  ·  HR Solutions On Call  ·  Last updated October 2026

Short answer: Small businesses without HR staff usually choose one of five options: do it yourself with free resources, hire an HR consultant by the hour or on retainer, join a PEO, subscribe to an HR outsourcing service like Bambee, or use an AI HR tool like YourHRCoach.ai. Many combine a low-cost daily tool with a consultant or attorney for high-risk issues.

Written and reviewed by Dr. Steve Cohen, Ed.D., founder of HR Solutions On Call, 40+ years of HR consulting · Last updated October 2026

Most businesses don't hire a dedicated HR person until they're well past 50 employees. Before that, HR lands on the owner, the office manager, or the bookkeeper — people who are good at their jobs but have never had to handle a harassment complaint or figure out whether a role is exempt from overtime.

You don't need a department. You need a reliable way to get the right answer when something comes up, and a way to keep the basics in order. Here are your real options, with honest tradeoffs. (Full disclosure: YourHRCoach.ai is our product. I'll tell you where it fits and where it doesn't.)

Can I just handle HR myself?

For a team of a few people with simple needs, yes — many owners do. Free, reliable sources include the U.S. Department of Labor (wage and hour rules), the EEOC (discrimination and harassment), your state labor department (final pay, leave, posters), and SHRM's public articles.

Pros: No cost beyond your time. You stay close to your people.

Cons: The problems that hurt small businesses — misclassifying employees as contractors, unpaid overtime for "salaried" staff, undocumented terminations — usually come from not knowing what you don't know. DIY also means nobody reviews your decision before you make it.

Choose DIY if: you have fewer than five employees, situations are routine, and you have someone to call when something serious happens.

Should I hire an HR consultant?

An independent HR consultant works by the hour, by project, or on a monthly retainer. Good ones bring judgment you can't get from a template: how to run a harassment investigation, how to structure a difficult termination, how to build a handbook that fits your state and culture.

Pros: Real expertise applied to your specific facts. Can meet with employees, investigate, and train managers in person.

Cons: Cost scales with use, so owners often hesitate to call for "small" questions — which is how small questions become big ones. Availability depends on the consultant's calendar.

Choose a consultant if: you're facing an investigation, a high-risk termination, a reorganization, or you want a custom handbook built by a human expert.

What is a PEO, and is it worth it?

A Professional Employer Organization (PEO) becomes the co-employer of your workers for payroll, tax, benefits, and workers' comp purposes. Because it pools many small employers, it can often offer better health insurance rates than you could get alone. Well-known PEOs include Justworks, Insperity, TriNet, and ADP TotalSource.

Pros: One provider for payroll, benefits, and compliance administration. Access to larger-group benefits. HR support is usually included.

Cons: Typically the most expensive option overall. You share some control over policies and benefits. Switching away later means re-establishing payroll, benefits, and tax accounts.

Choose a PEO if: better benefits are a priority and you want payroll and HR administration fully bundled.

What about HR outsourcing services like Bambee?

Subscription HR services assign you an HR manager (or team) who helps set up policies, handle employee issues, and answer questions. Bambee is the best-known example; others include Mineral (often bundled through payroll and insurance partners) and various local outsourced-HR firms.

Pros: A human HR contact without a full-time hire. Help building core policies.

How do I choose the right HR option for my business?

  1. List your last six months of HR issues. Mostly routine questions? Or investigations and disputes?
  2. Separate administration from advice. Payroll and benefits administration (Gusto, a PEO) is a different need from knowing what to do about an employee (consultant, AI tool, outsourced HR).
  3. Price your actual usage. A retainer you rarely use is expensive; an hourly consultant you avoid calling is risky.
  4. Plan for the high-stakes moments. Know which employment attorney you'd call before you need one.
  5. Revisit as you grow. Many businesses consider a dedicated HR hire somewhere between 50 and 100 employees. Also note that more federal laws kick in as you grow — for example, Title VII at 15 employees, COBRA at 20, and FMLA at 50.

What's a sensible HR setup for a small business?

A combination that works for many of the owners I've advised:

  • A payroll provider (or PEO) that handles pay, taxes, and required filings
  • A current employee handbook and written job descriptions
  • An everyday source of HR guidance for questions as they come up
  • A human HR consultant or employment attorney on call for high-risk situations
  • A habit of documenting performance issues and decisions in writing

Frequently Asked Questions

At what size does a small business need an HR department?

There's no legal threshold. Many businesses consider a dedicated HR hire somewhere between 50 and 100 employees. Below that, most use a mix of payroll software, outside advice, and the owner or office manager handling HR tasks.

What's the cheapest way to get HR help?

Free government resources (DOL, EEOC, your state labor department) cost nothing but time. Among paid options, a flat-rate AI HR tool like YourHRCoach.ai at $99/month is typically less expensive than ongoing consultant retainers or PEO fees, though it doesn't replace legal counsel.

What's the difference between a PEO and HR outsourcing?

A PEO becomes a co-employer and handles payroll, benefits, and workers' comp along with HR support. HR outsourcing services like Bambee provide HR guidance and policy help but generally don't become your co-employer or run your benefits.

Can an office manager handle HR?

Yes, many small businesses assign HR to an office manager. The key is giving them a reliable source of guidance and clear escalation rules for serious issues like harassment complaints, injuries, or terminations.

Is YourHRCoach.ai a PEO or payroll provider?

No. YourHRCoach.ai provides HR guidance and document generation. It doesn't run payroll, administer benefits, or become a co-employer, so it pairs with whatever payroll provider you already use.

When should I call an employment attorney instead?

Call an attorney when you receive an agency charge (EEOC or state), a demand letter or lawsuit, when terminating someone who recently complained or took protected leave, or when drafting a severance agreement and release.

Do I need an employee handbook if I don't have HR?

It isn't federally required, but a handbook is one of the most useful protections a small business can have. It sets expectations, documents policies like anti-harassment and leave, and supports consistent decisions.
